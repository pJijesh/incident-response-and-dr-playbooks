# Ransomware tabletop exercise pack

A discussion-based exercise in which a team talks through its response to a realistic ransomware incident, with timed injects (new information) delivered by a facilitator. No systems are touched and nothing is simulated technically. The value comes from finding gaps in decisions, roles, communications and recovery assumptions in a safe setting.

The scenario is entirely fictional: **Fernhill Freight Ltd**, a made-up freight forwarder of about 120 people with two offices and a bonded warehouse. Any resemblance to a real organisation is unintended. Adapt the details to your own environment before you run it.

## Objectives

By the end of the exercise, participants will have tested whether they can:

1. **Recognise and declare** a major incident quickly, and activate the right playbook and roles.
2. **Contain** a spreading attack while preserving evidence and protecting backups.
3. **Communicate** clearly and consistently to staff, customers, the board and regulators, using out-of-band channels.
4. **Decide** under uncertainty: external help, shutting systems, ransom demand, and notifications, with decisions logged.
5. **Meet legal and contractual clocks**, especially the 72-hour ICO assessment, insurer notification and customer obligations.
6. **Plan recovery** in BIA tier order, and understand backup and identity restore realities.

## At a glance

- Time: 2 hours (10 minutes introduction, 95 minutes of injects, 15 minutes hot debrief)
- Group size: 6 to 12 participants, plus a facilitator, an inject controller and an observer or scribe
- Format: round table (in person or video call), printed handouts
- Preparation: about 2 hours for the facilitator
- Output: completed evaluation forms, a decision log, and a [lessons-learned record](lessons-learned-template.md) with owned actions

## Safety notes

- Mark every document **EXERCISE** and use fictional names and numbers only.
- Do not use real credentials, real customer names or real incident data.
- Do not email exercise injects from real accounts to real third parties.
- Tell staff, suppliers and any external responders in advance, so that nobody mistakes the exercise for a real incident.
